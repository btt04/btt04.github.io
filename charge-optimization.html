The problem

A charge is a mix of materials, and each one brings its own chemistry and its own cost. The metal has a target chemistry, element by element. The question every day is the same: what is the cheapest mix that lands on target with what is actually available?

That is an optimization problem whether or not anyone writes it down as one. I wrote it down.

Pricing the miss instead of forbidding it

The obvious formulation treats target chemistry as hard constraints: minimize cost, subject to every element landing inside its window. The model instead minimizes cost plus a squared-deviation penalty from target, with a separate weight on each element.

  • It always returns an answer. On a day when the available materials cannot hit every window at once, a hard-constraint model just reports that it is infeasible. A priced penalty returns the best achievable charge and shows where it falls short.
  • Squaring the deviation punishes big misses disproportionately. Being a little off on several elements costs less than being far off on one.
  • Per-element weights encode what matters. An element whose excursion causes real trouble downstream carries a heavier weight than one with more tolerance.

Hard constraints tell you when the charge can’t hit spec. A priced penalty tells you what it would cost to get closer.

Why Multistart

GRG Nonlinear is a local method. It walks downhill from wherever it starts and stops in the nearest valley, which is not necessarily the deepest one. Multistart runs it from many starting points and keeps the best result — slower, but it stops the answer depending on where the solver happened to begin.

Sweeping the penalty

The weights are a dial between cost and precision. Turn them up and the model chases target chemistry harder and spends more to do it; turn them down and it saves money at the expense of drift. Automating penalty sweeps in VBA makes that trade-off visible instead of leaving it as a guess about what the weights ought to be.

The daily solve is automated the same way, so the model is a tool that runs rather than a spreadsheet someone has to remember to open.
