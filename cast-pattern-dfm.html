Project 07 — CadQuery — 2026

Parametric Cast Iron Pattern

The model looked correct from every angle. It was also unmanufacturable, and the reason had nothing to do with how thick I had made anything.

Tool
CadQuery — scripted CAD
Defect
0.5 mm knife edge
Corrected to
4.0 mm uniform wall
Method
Lofted bore tracking valley radius
Allowances
Shrinkage, draw direction

The defect

A scalloped pan is a good test case for scripted CAD. The form is defined by a handful of numbers — scallop count, scallop radius, bore diameter, overall height — and it is exactly the kind of part where changing one of them by hand in a GUI means rebuilding half the model.

The straightforward construction — revolve the scalloped outer profile, then subtract a cylindrical central bore — produces a part that looks right and cannot be made.

The scallops are a wave. Their valleys come closer to the centerline than their peaks do. A cylindrical bore is a constant radius. Where the deepest point of a scallop valley meets that bore, the wall between them collapsed to 0.5 mm — a knife edge.

That is a defect you cannot see by rotating the model, because from the outside the part looks fine. It only appears when you ask the geometry a manufacturing question: what is the thinnest section anywhere in this solid, and can the process fill it?

The fix

The answer was not to thicken the part or open up the scallops, both of which change the design to suit the tool. It was to stop treating the bore as a cylinder.

I lofted the bore so that it tracks the scallop valley radius at a constant offset. The bore now waves in sympathy with the outer surface instead of ignoring it, and the wall between them is a uniform 4.0 mm everywhere rather than varying between comfortable and impossible.

The thin wall was never really a thickness problem. It was two surfaces defined in unrelated ways, and the fix was to define one of them in terms of the other.

Designing the pattern, not the part

The pattern is not the finished object, and its dimensions are not the drawing's dimensions. Two allowances went into the model:

  • Shrinkage — the pattern is made deliberately oversized so the part lands on nominal once it has cooled and contracted.
  • Draw direction — every surface has to release cleanly along a single axis, which constrains what the scallop geometry is allowed to do near the parting line.

Because the whole thing is parametric, both allowances are variables rather than baked-in numbers. Changing the alloy or the process means changing one value and re-running the script, not remodeling the pan.
