Eagle Motorsports

Across three roles on the Baja team — frame lead, two years on drivetrain, then captain — I machined suspension and driveline parts: one-piece billet knuckles, rear bearing carriers, heim reducers, spacers, and bushings, on the HAAS lathes, the UMC 750 five-axis, and wire and sinker EDM.

Staying that long meant living with my own designs. I saw them through several full cycles — designed, made, run, broken, redesigned — which is a different education from designing a part once and handing it off.

Holding the number

I turned a tensile test bar to 16.000 mm on a HAAS ST-10 — after re-leveling the lathe first.

Gears, start to finish

I made the gears for the Baja drivetrain and developed the heat treat processes for them — pack carburizing, quench and temper, normalizing, and annealing. Cutting a gear is half the job; the case depth and core hardness decide whether it survives.

When the part fights back

At Hendrickson, a batch of castings arrived at the lathe hard. The symptom was on the machine; the cause was not. I worked out that the castings had not been inoculated.

That still left parts on the lathe. The instinct on a hard part is to slow everything down. I cut the carbide surface speed, but held the feed: back the feed off too, and the tool stops cutting and starts rubbing.

That salvaged the parts. It did not fix anything. The real fix was upstream in the melt — the machining change only kept the problem from turning into scrap.

The lathe is where the problem showed up, which is not the same thing as where it started.

Pens

Titanium and brass. Titanium is the more interesting of the two — it holds heat at the cutting edge, work hardens, and deflects, so a clean finish on it is harder won than it looks.

Test specimens

As an undergraduate research assistant, I machined tensile samples on the CNC lathe for graduate students’ research.
